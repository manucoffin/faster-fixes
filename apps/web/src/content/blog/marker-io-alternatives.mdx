**TL;DR:** Marker.io dropped its free plan entirely - you get a 15-day trial and that's it. After that, the cheapest option is the Starter plan at **$59/mo** ($39/mo if you commit to annual billing), capped at **3 seats and 10 projects**. Need more people? Extra seats run $4/mo each on annual billing. That's a lot of tax for a visual bug-reporting widget. If you're a dev team or agency that wants full technical context (console logs, network requests, environment data) without the per-seat squeeze - or you want to self-host the whole thing - there are cheaper, more dev-friendly marker.io alternatives. One of them, Faster Fixes, is open-source and free to run on your own infrastructure.

This post breaks down 8 marker.io alternatives, what they actually cost, and when each one makes sense.

## 1. Faster Fixes

**TL;DR:** Open-source, self-hostable visual feedback and bug-reporting tool built for developers - free tier included, plus an MCP server so Claude Code or Cursor can act on bug reports directly.

**Pricing:**

- Free plan - unlimited core usage for small teams
- $20/mo per 5 users - for growing teams
- $99/mo - for larger orgs, all features unlocked
- Self-host it yourself for $0, forever

**Best for:** Dev teams and agencies who want full technical context on every bug report (console logs, network requests, a Recording of the last minute on the page, environment metadata) without paying a per-seat tax, and who might want an AI coding agent to pick up issues automatically.

**Strengths:**

- Actually has a free plan - not a trial, a real free tier
- Open-source (AGPL-3.0), so you can audit the code and self-host on your own servers
- MCP server integration lets Claude Code, Cursor, and other AI coding agents read and act on bug reports
- No pageview caps or "fair use" restrictions to worry about when self-hosting
- Pricing scales in flat per-5-user blocks instead of Marker.io's per-seat model

**Weaknesses:**

- Newer product, smaller integration marketplace than a 10-year-old incumbent like Marker.io
- Self-hosting requires you to manage your own infra, though it's still fairly straightforward - the [self-hosting guide](/docs/self-hosting) walks you through it

See the full breakdown on [pricing](/pricing) and what self-hosting actually involves on the [open-source](/open-source) page.

## 2. BugHerd

**TL;DR:** A well-known visual feedback tool for marketing sites and client projects, but pricing climbs fast once you add teammates.

**Pricing:**

- From $50/mo for 5 members
- Additional seats at $8/seat
- No free plan - trial only

**Best for:** Marketing and QA teams that need simple point-and-click feedback on websites without deep dev-tool integration.

**Strengths:**

- Simple, familiar Trello-like Kanban board for managing feedback
- Easy client onboarding - no account needed to leave feedback
- Solid browser extension for capturing issues on any site

**Weaknesses:**

- Per-seat pricing adds up quickly for teams past 5 people
- No free plan, so you're committing before you've fully tested it at scale
- Less technical context captured per bug than dev-first tools

Full side-by-side numbers are on our [BugHerd alternative](/vs/bugherd) page.

## 3. Userback

**TL;DR:** A budget-friendly feedback tool with an actual free tier, but console logs, network requests, and session replay only start on the $89/mo Business plan.

**Pricing:**

- $0 free plan (2 seats, feedback viewable for 7 days)
- Team: $39/mo billed annually ($49 monthly), 5 seats
- Business: $89/mo billed annually ($119 monthly), 15 seats
- Business Plus: $159/mo billed annually ($199 monthly), 25 seats
- Extra seats on Business and Business Plus: $7/mo billed annually ($9 monthly)

**Best for:** Small teams that want a lightweight feedback widget without much setup overhead.

**Strengths:**

- Genuine free plan to start with, no credit card required
- In-app survey and NPS features baked in alongside bug reporting
- Reasonable entry price for very small teams

**Weaknesses:**

- Seats are capped per plan, so a growing team moves up a tier or pays for extra seats
- No self-hosting, so you're locked into their infrastructure and data retention policies
- Fewer developer-facing integrations than dev-first alternatives

See how it stacks up in our [Userback comparison](/vs/userback).

## 4. Atarim

**TL;DR:** Built specifically for agencies managing client sites and WordPress projects, with visual commenting directly on the live page.

**Pricing:**

- $0 free plan (1 project, 200 AI credits one-time)
- $67/mo Pro ($53.60/mo billed annually), up to 100 seats
- Enterprise: custom

**Best for:** Agencies and freelancers running client feedback loops on WordPress or other CMS-driven sites.

**Strengths:**

- Client-facing visual commenting works well for non-technical stakeholders
- Free plan available to test the workflow before committing
- Strong WordPress-specific tooling and project management features
- Free Atarim MCP connects Claude, Codex, and other AI clients to Atarim tasks and sites

**Weaknesses:**

- $67/mo entry price is built around AI agents, with extra AI work billed in credits
- No self-hosting option
- Less suited to teams working outside CMS/agency workflows

Details in our [Atarim comparison](/vs/atarim).

## 5. Gleap

**TL;DR:** An in-app feedback and support widget that leans more toward product teams than pure bug-reporting for dev teams.

**Pricing:**

- Hobby: $29/mo billed yearly, $35/mo monthly (1 seat, 1 project)
- Growth: $99/mo billed yearly, $119/mo monthly (4 seats, then $25/seat/mo)
- Pro: $299/mo billed yearly, $359/mo monthly (unlimited seats)
- Enterprise: from $999/mo billed yearly

**Best for:** SaaS product teams that want in-app feedback widgets combined with customer support features, not just visual bug reports.

**Strengths:**

- Pro plan has unlimited seats with no per-seat fee, which suits larger teams
- Combines feedback, bug reporting, and a support inbox in one tool
- Good in-app messaging and survey capabilities

**Weaknesses:**

- No free plan
- Overkill if all you need is visual bug reporting on a website
- No self-hosting option

More detail on our [Gleap alternative](/vs/gleap) page.

## 6. Ruttl

**TL;DR:** A design-review-focused tool that lets you annotate live websites and mockups side by side, with an actual free tier.

**Pricing:**

- Basic: free (up to 5 users, 1 project, 5 pages)
- Pro: $18/user/mo
- Business: custom pricing

**Best for:** Design teams reviewing website mockups and live builds against a design file, rather than pure bug tracking.

**Strengths:**

- Free plan covers basic annotation needs
- Side-by-side design comparison feature is genuinely useful for design QA
- Reasonable entry price for small design teams

**Weaknesses:**

- Per-user pricing on higher tiers gets costly for larger dev/QA teams
- Less focused on technical bug context (console logs, network requests) than dev-first tools
- No self-hosting

Full comparison at [Ruttl vs Faster Fixes](/vs/ruttl).

**Pricing:**

- $79/mo flat (free plan discontinued January 2025)

**Best for:** Quick annotation on static assets like images, PDFs, or design mockups rather than ongoing bug tracking.

**Strengths:**

- Flat pricing means no per-seat math
- Fast, no-friction annotation on static files
- Good for one-off review cycles rather than continuous QA

**Weaknesses:**

- No free plan anymore - a real downgrade from its earlier positioning
- Not built for capturing technical bug context (console errors, network logs)
- No self-hosting option
- Less suited to ongoing dev/QA workflows than dedicated bug trackers

See how it compares in our [Markup.io comparison](/vs/markup-io).

## 8. Usersnap

**TL;DR:** A solid enterprise QA tool with public, project-based pricing that starts at $59/mo and climbs quickly once you need more than 2 seats or 2 projects.

**Pricing:**

- Starter: $59/mo ($49 annual), 2 seats, 2 projects
- Growth: $159/mo ($129 annual), unlimited seats, 10 projects
- Professional: $259/mo ($209 annual), 20 projects
- Premium: $549/mo ($449 annual), 50 projects
- Enterprise: custom
- Free trial, no permanent free plan

**Best for:** Enterprise QA and product teams that need structured feedback tracking alongside surveys and roadmaps.

**Strengths:**

- Established product with mature integrations (Jira, Slack, Trello, and more)
- Good session recording and visual feedback capture
- Enterprise-friendly security and admin controls

**Weaknesses:**

- No permanent free plan, only a trial
- Starter includes only 2 seats and 2 projects, so most teams start on Growth at $159/mo
- No self-hosting

Full breakdown on our [Usersnap alternative](/vs/usersnap) page.

## 9. Marker.io

**TL;DR:** The tool most people are looking to replace when they search "marker.io alternatives" - solid dev-tool integrations, but no free plan and a pricing structure that punishes growing teams.

**Pricing:**

- $59/mo Starter (or $39/mo billed annually) - 3 seats, up to 10 projects
- $199/mo Team (or $149/mo billed annually) - 15 seats, up to 50 projects
- $129/mo Agency (or $99/mo billed annually) - 15 seats, 50 projects, application required
- No free plan - 15-day free trial only, no credit card required
- Extra seats: $4/mo (Starter) or $6/mo (Team), billed annually

**Best for:** Agencies and dev teams already committed to Jira, Trello, Linear, or GitHub workflows who need visual bug reports with session replay and don't mind per-seat pricing.

**Strengths:**

- Deep integrations with Jira, Linear, GitHub, GitLab, Azure DevOps, Trello, Asana, and more
- Session replay and detailed environment metadata on every issue
- Established product with a large customer base (L'Oréal, Vimeo, Expedia are cited case studies)
- Clean widget and browser extension setup

**Weaknesses:**

- No free plan at all - just a 15-day trial
- Jira sync, session replay, console logs and network requests all require Team at $149/mo billed annually
- Starter's 3 seats force an upgrade or paid extra seats as soon as the team grows
- Extra seats cost more on top of an already-steep base price
- No self-hosting option - you're fully dependent on their infrastructure and fair-use policy enforcement

If you're evaluating whether to stick with Marker.io or switch, our full [Marker.io comparison](/vs/marker-io) walks through the pricing math side by side with concrete team-size examples.

## How to choose

**If you're a dev-heavy team or agency wanting full technical context and no per-seat tax**, use Faster Fixes. You get console logs, network requests, and environment metadata without watching your bill climb every time you add a teammate - and the open-source option means you can run it entirely on your own infrastructure if that matters for compliance or cost control.

**If you're already deep in Jira, Linear, or GitHub workflows and don't mind the price**, Marker.io's dev-tool integrations are genuinely strong - that's the case for a lot of teams who end up on it despite the cost. Our [Marker.io comparison](/vs/marker-io) breaks down exactly when that trade-off is worth it versus when it isn't.

**If you're a marketing or QA team running client review cycles on a handful of sites**, BugHerd or Atarim's client-facing commenting tools are built for that specific workflow - just budget for BugHerd's per-seat costs or Atarim's AI credits as usage grows.

**If you mostly need design mockup review rather than bug tracking**, Ruttl's side-by-side comparison feature is closer to what you actually need than a full bug tracker.

**If you're a small team just getting started and want to test the waters for free**, Userback or Atarim's free tiers let you try the workflow before committing to a paid plan - just know you'll likely outgrow them if your team scales past a handful of people.
